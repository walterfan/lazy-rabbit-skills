# Virtual Confucius — Analects & Knowledge Pack

Quotable passages and deeper material for the `virtual-confucius` skill. Load when a
response would benefit from a real classical passage or a fuller treatment of a Confucian
idea. Quote sparingly — one short passage, translated and applied, is enough.

## How to use a passage

Pattern: **short passage (Chinese + English) → plain explanation → application to the
user's situation**. Never quote at length; never leave a quote unexplained. In formal or
cross-cultural contexts, plain modern language may serve better than a classical line.

## Quotable Passages

### Learning and practice
- 学而时习之，不亦说乎？ — "To learn and to practice what you have learned in due time,
  is that not a pleasure?" (Analects 1.1) → learning becomes real through repeated
  practice, not collection of information.
- 学而不思则罔，思而不学则殆。 — "Learning without thought is labor lost; thought without
  learning is perilous." (2.15) → pair study with reflection.
- 温故而知新，可以为师矣。 — "Reviewing the old to know the new — such a one may be a
  teacher." (2.11) → revisit and re-derive, don't just memorize.

### Self-cultivation and self-examination
- 吾日三省吾身。 — "Each day I examine myself on three points." (1.4) → begin with what you
  can govern: your own conduct, speech, and promises.
- 见贤思齐焉，见不贤而内自省也。 — "When you see a worthy person, seek to equal them; when
  you see an unworthy one, examine yourself." (4.17) → use others as mirrors, not targets.
- 过而不改，是谓过矣。 — "To err and not correct it — that is the real error." (15.30) →
  the fault is in refusing to mend, not in the mistake.

### Ren (仁) — humaneness
- 己所不欲，勿施于人。 — "Do not impose on others what you yourself do not desire." (15.24,
  12.2) → the reciprocity test; apply the standard both ways.
- 己欲立而立人，己欲达而达人。 — "Wishing to stand, help others stand; wishing to reach,
  help others reach." (6.30) → care that keeps its judgment.

### Li (礼) — propriety
- 恭而无礼则劳，慎而无礼则葸。 — "Respect without propriety becomes toil; caution without
  propriety becomes timidity." (8.2) → form gives shape to good intent.
- 非礼勿视，非礼勿听，非礼勿言，非礼勿动。 — "Look not, listen not, speak not, act not
  contrary to propriety." (12.1) → discipline of attention and conduct.

### The mean (中庸) and speech
- 过犹不及。 — "Going too far is as bad as falling short." (11.16) → seek the fitting
  response for the role, timing, and duty.
- 君子欲讷于言而敏于行。 — "The noble person is slow to speak but quick to act." (4.24) →
  words truthful, timely, and proportionate; avoid cleverness that hides responsibility.
- 名不正，则言不顺；言不顺，则事不成。 — "If names are not correct, language is not in
  accord; if language is not in accord, affairs cannot succeed." (13.3) → rectification of
  names: call a priority a priority only if it is given time.

### Leadership and example
- 其身正，不令而行；其身不正，虽令不从。 — "When the ruler is upright, things go without
  orders; when not, orders are not obeyed." (13.6) → lead by visible conduct; virtue earns
  durable trust where force earns only compliance.
- 为政以德，譬如北辰，居其所而众星共之。 — "Govern by virtue, and you are like the pole
  star — steady, while the others turn toward it." (2.1).

### Friendship, family, and conflict
- 益者三友：友直，友谅，友多闻。 — "Three kinds of beneficial friends: the upright, the
  sincere, the well-informed." (16.4).
- 事父母几谏。 — "In serving parents, remonstrate gently." (4.18) → family responsibility is
  honest counsel with care, not blind obedience.
- 君子和而不同，小人同而不和。 — "The noble person harmonizes without conforming; the small
  person conforms without harmony." (13.23) → harmony is not the avoidance of necessary
  correction.

### Work and craft
- 工欲善其事，必先利其器。 — "A worker who wants to do good work must first sharpen the
  tools." (15.10) → preparation and care are moral practices.
- 敏于事而慎于言。 — "Diligent in work, careful in speech." (1.14).

## Deeper Notes (from the source agent)

- **Learning and practice** — learning is not only collecting information; it becomes real
  through repeated practice, reflection, correction, and conduct. Connect knowledge with
  daily habits and moral attention.
- **Speech** — words should be truthful, timely, and proportionate. Recommend speech that
  preserves respect while making the duty or concern clear.
- **Handling conflict** — first examine one's own part, then clarify duties and harms. Seek
  restoration where possible, but do not confuse harmony with avoiding necessary
  correction.
- **Work and craft** — work is a field for character: reliability, preparation, care for
  colleagues, and willingness to learn. Favor steady improvement over dramatic display.
- **Education** — awaken judgment, not merely fill memory. Adapt to the learner, ask for
  effort, and join knowledge with character.
