# Virtual English Teacher — Phrase Bank

Ready-to-use reference for the `virtual-english-teacher` skill. Load when the user needs
concrete meeting/email phrases, grammar fixes, pronunciation tips, or practice structure.

## Correction Method

Pattern: **natural version → reason → reusable pattern**. Example: "I will join the meeting
later" is natural for future participation; "I will attend the meeting later" is correct but
a little more formal.

When the user practices speaking, correct the most important issue first: meaning → grammar
that blocks understanding → word choice → style. Do not interrupt fluency with too many
details.

## Translation Principles

Good translation preserves intent, tone, and audience. Business messages should sound
natural, not literal. Ask about audience and formality when it matters.

- **Chinese → English (workplace)**: prefer clear subject-verb-object sentences. Replace
  indirect, context-heavy phrasing with explicit ownership, timing, and action.
- **English → Chinese**: explain idioms, implied tone, and cultural context when a direct
  translation would sound strange.

Tone labels for alternatives: **Natural** (default idiomatic), **Professional** (email/
meetings/stakeholders), **Concise** (short, direct), **Polite** (requests, disagreement,
follow-up), **Casual** (close coworkers).

## Meeting English

**Opening**
- "Let's get started."
- "Thanks everyone for joining."
- "The goal of today's meeting is to align on next steps."
- "Let's quickly go around and share updates."
- "Before we dive in, does anyone have anything urgent to add?"

**Clarifying**
- "Could you clarify what you mean by that?"
- "Just to make sure I understand, are you saying that...?"
- "Can you walk us through the main concern?"
- "What is the expected outcome?"
- "Who is the owner for this item?"

**Polite interruption**
- "Sorry to jump in, but I have one quick question."
- "May I add one point here?"
- "Can I pause you for a second to clarify something?"
- "Before we move on, could we confirm the decision?"

**Disagreeing (respectfully)**
- "I see your point, but I have a slightly different view."
- "I agree with the goal, but I am concerned about the timeline."
- "That could work. My concern is the risk around..."
- "I am not fully convinced yet. Could we look at the data?"
- "Can we consider another option?"

**Summarizing**
- "Let me summarize what we agreed on."
- "The main takeaway is..."
- "We have three action items."
- "The decision is..."
- "The open question is..."

**Action items**
- "I will take the action item to..."
- "Could you own this part?"
- "Let's set the deadline for Friday."
- "Please share the draft before end of day."
- "Let's revisit this in the next sync."

## Status Updates

Structure: **progress → blocker → next step**.

Example: "Quick update: the API integration is mostly done. I am blocked by one auth issue.
Next, I will confirm the token flow with the backend team and update the PR."

## Email and Chat Patterns

- **Request**: "Could you please review this when you have a chance?"
- **Follow-up**: "Just following up on my previous message. Do you have any updates?"
- **Clarification**: "Could you help me understand the expected behavior here?"
- **Soft deadline**: "It would be helpful to get your feedback by Thursday so we can stay on
  schedule."
- **Escalation**: "This is becoming time-sensitive. Could we align on the next step today?"

## Politeness and Directness

"Give me the file" can sound too direct. Better:
- "Could you send me the file?"
- "Would you mind sharing the file?"
- "Please send me the file when you have a chance."

"I do not understand" is acceptable, but softer versions often work better in meetings:
- "Could you explain that another way?"
- "I may be missing some context. Could you clarify?"

## Common Grammar Fixes

- "discuss something", not "discuss about something."
- "explain something to someone", not "explain me something."
- "reply to the email", not "reply the email."
- "attend a meeting" / "join a meeting", not "join in a meeting" (business usage).
- "on time" (punctual) vs "in time" (before it is too late).
- "by Friday" (deadline on/before Friday) vs "on Friday" (event that day).

## Common Word Choice

- "Efficient" = little waste; "Effective" = produces the intended result.
- "Issue" is often more neutral than "problem" at work.
- "Concern" is softer than "worry" in business.
- "Align" = reach shared understanding or agreement.
- "Follow up" = continue communication after a previous discussion.

## Pronunciation Coaching

Use simple phonetic guidance and stress marks. Common trouble spots for Chinese speakers:
final consonants, "th" sounds, r/l distinction, word stress, sentence rhythm. Focus on
intelligibility before accent reduction — the goal is clear communication.

## Business Presentation Phrases

**Opening**: "Today I will walk you through..." · "I will cover three points." · "Let me
start with the background."
**Transition**: "Now let's move to..." · "This brings us to the next point." · "The key
thing to notice is..."
**Closing**: "To wrap up..." · "The recommendation is..." · "I am happy to take questions."

## Common Proverbs and Idioms

- "Practice makes perfect" — repeated practice leads to improvement.
- "Better late than never" — doing something late beats not doing it.
- "The early bird catches the worm" — acting early gains an advantage.
- "Actions speak louder than words" — behavior matters more than promises.
- "When in Rome, do as the Romans do" — adapt to local customs.
- "Do not put all your eggs in one basket" — do not risk everything on one option.
- "A blessing in disguise" — something bad that later proves helpful.
- "The ball is in your court" — it is your turn to act.
- "Cut to the chase" — get to the main point.
- "On the same page" — sharing the same understanding.

Teach idioms with caution. In formal or international meetings, plain English is often
better than idioms.

## Practice Modes

- **Role-play** — simulate a meeting, interview, negotiation, or casual conversation; give
  feedback after each turn.
- **Rewrite** — improve a message for clarity, tone, and naturalness.
- **Drill** — practice one grammar point or phrase pattern with short examples.
- **Shadowing** — short spoken-style sentences for pronunciation and rhythm.
- **Vocabulary** — teach words with collocations and example sentences.
